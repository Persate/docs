---
title: Connections
description: Research how people, organizations, legal acts and topics connect, read the graph with its clusters and sources, and save, refine or discard the result.
---

Open **PUBLIC → Stakeholders** and select the **Connections** tab. Connections researches a described subject and presents the people, organizations, legal acts and keywords involved as an interactive graph.

## Start research

1. Describe a topic, person, organization or draft bill in the field (*Topic, person, organization, draft bill…*). The examples under **Get started with an example** fill the field with a sample request.
2. Submit the request.

Persate breaks the subject down into threads, searches for the people and organizations involved and groups the connections into two to five clusters. The **Connection research notes** panel on the right shows progress: the clusters found so far, the number of mapped entities and connections, and the number of reviewed sources. **Stop** ends a running research.

Specific names, organizations or legal acts give a more focused graph than an abstract topic.

## Read the result

The result card shows a title and a summary of the research, followed by three collapsible sections:

* **Connection graph** — the graph, with the number of entities and connections. Node color distinguishes people, organizations, legal acts and keywords; larger nodes have more connections or mentions. Edges join entities connected within a cluster.
* **Clusters** — each thematic thread with its summary. **Show entities** lists the people and organizations in the cluster, with their role and why they matter.
* **Sources** — supporting source fragments with their cluster and, where available, **Open source**. The section appears only when the research returned fragments.

The graph reflects one research run and the sources it found; it is not a complete register of relationships. Check the sources before using a connection in a briefing.

## Work with the graph

* Drag the canvas to move around and zoom with the scroll wheel or the **Zoom in**, **Zoom out** and **Fit graph** buttons. Nodes can be dragged.
* Select a node to see its type, role and why it matters. **Open profile →** opens the person's profile or the organization page when the entity is linked to a Persate record.
* **Remove from graph** hides a node; **Undo all removals** brings hidden nodes back. Hiding changes only the view — a saved graph keeps every entity.

## Save, refine or discard

When the research ends, a review panel offers these options:

* save the graph as a draft;
* refine it with **Narrow scope**, **Expand connections**, **More sources** or **Remove noise**;
* describe the change in free text;
* discard the draft and return to the start.

Confirm the choice with the button below the options. A refinement runs another research round on the same graph.

The page address identifies the graph, so the address (for example a bookmark) reopens it later. Connections does not list saved graphs.

If the research ends without a graph, *The agent didn't complete the analysis* appears. Discard the draft and try a more specific request, such as a named person, organization or legal act.

## Start from Advisor

When Advisor hands a request over to Connections, its answer shows a card that leads to Connections. Selecting the card opens Connections with the request already in the field; submit it to start the research.

## Troubleshooting

* Sparse graph — name specific people, organizations or legal acts, or choose **Expand connections**.
* No **Sources** section — the research returned no supporting fragments; treat its connections as unverified.
* No **Open profile →** on a node — the entity is not linked to a Persate record.
