Every example contains:

* **Query** — the content of the entered message.
* **What the Advisor does** — the skill families and tool chips that appear in the activity.
* **What the response looks like** — the structure of the answer and its citations.

The actual content of every response varies, and chip labels can change as tools are updated. These are only examples; the general shape of the response and the tools used generally remain similar. In the sample responses, a resource URI in square brackets stands for a citation chip — in the product, the chip shows the source's title and opens the source.

## Example 1 — Votes

**Query:**

> Which Civic Coalition MPs voted against the proposed amendment to the energy bill at the last Sejm sitting? Show the result and link the vote.

**What the Advisor does:**

1. **`legislation`** — lists the votes of the last sitting and selects those on the energy bill and the amendment (`Listing votings`).
2. **`legislation`** — reads the selected vote: its result and how each MP voted (`Reading voting`).
3. **`stakeholders`** — matches the names to MP profiles (`Searching legislators`).

**What the response looks like:**

> The amendment was **rejected** in vote 47 at sitting 12 on 28 March 2026, by 198 for and 232 against — see [legislation://voting/12/47]. The following Civic Coalition MPs voted against: [feature://stakeholder/abc-123], [feature://stakeholder/def-456], [feature://stakeholder/ghi-789] (3 of the club's 134 members).

## Example 2 — Public stance versus voting record

**Query:**

> The Climate Coalition has recently been vocal about phasing out coal. How did its members actually vote on coal-related amendments in the last month? Are there any discrepancies?

**What the Advisor does:**

1. **`stakeholders`** — finds the club's MPs (`Searching legislators`).
2. **`public_pulse`** — searches the club's posts from the last month on the coal phase-out (`Searching tweets`).
3. **`civic`** — finds coal-related votes in the same period (`Searching votings by topic`).
4. **`legislation`** — reads each candidate vote (`Reading voting`).
5. **`legislation`** — cross-checks the individual MPs' votes (`Votes by legislator`).

**What the response looks like:**

> Out of nine votes on coal amendments in the last 30 days, the Coalition voted together in six. Three discrepancies:
>
> * **MP Kowalska** ([feature://stakeholder/...]) wrote publicly in favor of the proposed transformation fund ([feature://public_pulse/tweet/...]), but **abstained** on the funding amendment ([legislation://voting/12/52]).
> * The Coalition split 7–2 on the mine closure schedule amendment ([legislation://voting/12/61]).
> * **MP Nowak** ([feature://stakeholder/...]) was absent for three of the nine votes.

## Example 3 — Analysis of an attached file

**Query:**

> Summarize the regulatory recommendations in this briefing and check whether any of them has progressed in Parliament. *(with `Q1-2026-renewables-briefing.pdf` attached through `@`)*

**What the Advisor does:**

1. **`documents`** — reads the attached file directly; no search is needed because the file is identified in the message (`Reading file`).
2. **`civic`** — looks for bills and prints that match each recommendation (`Search processes`, `Searching parliamentary prints`).
3. **`legislation`** — reads the related votes (`Reading voting`).

**What the response looks like:**

> The briefing contains five recommendations [documents://chunk/.../...]. Among them:
>
> * **Recommendation 2** (simplifying the grid connection permit process) corresponds to amendment 14 to the energy bill, *passed* on 22 March — see [legislation://voting/12/41].
> * **Recommendation 4** (raising the contract-for-difference limit) was tabled but *withdrawn* before the vote [documents://chunk/.../...].
> * The remaining three recommendations have not appeared in any bill so far.

The file chips open the briefing in the file viewer at the cited passage.

## Example 4 — Preparing an alert with a clarifying question

**Query:**

> Prepare an alert for new developments on the pharmaceutical reform bill.

**What the Advisor does:**

1. **`civic`** — looks for the bill the request refers to (`Search processes`).
2. Two bills match the description, so the Advisor asks which one is meant (`Asking clarifying question`); the candidates appear in the conversation as options.
3. *(one option is chosen and confirmed with **Continue**)*
4. **`alerts`** — checks whether an existing alert already covers the bill (`Listing alerts`).
5. **`agent`** — saves an inactive alert draft for the Tracker (`Prepare workspace`).

**What the response looks like:**

> I prepared an alert draft, **Pharmaceutical reform**, for the bill selected above. Monitoring has not started: review the scope and sources in the Tracker and save the alert to activate it.

Below the answer, a **Monitoring setup** card with the status **Setup required · Monitoring is not active** opens the alert creator with the draft. If the conversation is still open when the draft is ready, Persate opens the creator automatically.

## Example 5 — What was said at a sitting

**Query:**

> At the latest Justice Committee sitting, where did the chair announce the schedule for the public procurement amendment? Quote the exact words.

**What the Advisor does:**

1. **`recordings`** — finds the latest analyzed recording of the committee (`Searching recordings catalog`).
2. **`recordings`** — searches the statements about the procurement amendment (`Searching speeches in recordings`).
3. **`recordings`** — reads the transcript around the matching statement (`Reading recording transcript`).

**What the response looks like:**

> The chair announced the schedule towards the end of the sitting [recordings://speech/.../...]. Quoted passage:
>
> *"The procurement-law amendment will be considered in second reading at the next sitting on 5 April."*

The chip opens the recording in **Recordings** at the cited moment.

## Example 6 — Multi-source legislative briefing

**Query:**

> I need a one-page brief on the renewable energy bill: legislative status, recent committee work, how the clubs have voted on it so far, and what they say publicly.

**What the Advisor does:**

1. **`agent`** — publishes a plan; its steps appear in the **Plan** panel (`Updating plan`).
2. **`agent`** — starts research agents for parts of the brief; each appears as a chip that opens its own session (`Research agents`).
3. **`civic`** — the bill's stages and prints (`Legislative process`, `Print history`).
4. **`recordings`** — committee discussions of the bill (`Searching speeches in recordings`).
5. **`legislation`** — how each club voted at earlier stages (`Reading voting`, `Votes by legislator`).
6. **`public_pulse`** — the public positions of the main clubs (`Searching tweets`).
7. **`alerts`** — whether an alert already covers the bill (`Listing alerts`).

**What the response looks like:**

> *Status.* The bill is in second reading; the committee report is available as a Sejm print ([legislation://print/10/1234]).
>
> *Recent committee work.* At the last committee sitting, the chair set the date of the next reading ([recordings://speech/.../...]).
>
> *Votes so far.* Votes on the amendments at earlier stages, broken down by club ([legislation://voting/12/38]).
>
> *Public positioning.* The position of each main club, with its posts ([feature://public_pulse/tweet/...]).
>
> No alert covers this bill yet — one can be prepared on request.

## Recurring patterns in the examples

Three patterns recur in the examples above:

* **The Advisor starts with structured records.** Votes, legislative processes and MP identities provide an anchor — text searches are narrowed by what the structured tools found.
* **Multi-source citations are the norm.** Even simple queries produce several citation chips, so every claim can be verified.
* **Large questions are planned and divided.** A broad request gets a plan and may be split among research agents; if the whole question cannot be covered in one turn, the answer says which part remains and suggests a narrower follow-up.
